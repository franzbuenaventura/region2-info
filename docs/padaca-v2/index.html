The Padaca Years

Isabela, 2004–2010

Maria Gracia Cielo “Grace” Magno Padaca — accountant, radio broadcaster, polio survivor — became the first governor of Isabela outside the Dy political family in roughly 35 years. This page lays out what her administration did, what went wrong, and what the courts later decided, next to the Dy administrations before and after hers.

  • Neutral · evidence-first — not a fan page
  • Every claim carries a source
  • Court conviction and criticisms included

In one minute

Who

A certified public accountant who spent 14 years as radio commentator “Bombo Grace” criticizing Dy-era corruption, illegal gambling, and logging — then ran for office after losing a 2001 congressional race decided by the tribunal at 48 votes.

RMAF · HRET · Bulatlat

What she did

Governed 2004–2010. Cut the province’s debt by two-thirds, expanded health coverage to ~700,000 residents, pushed hybrid rice, and led the biggest illegal-timber seizure in Philippine history. Won the Ramon Magsaysay Award in 2008.

RMAF · PCIJ · GMA

What the courts decided

Convicted in 2019 of malversation over a ₱25M rice-program loan to an NGO (₱18M never accounted for) — upheld on reconsideration. Her graft conviction was acquitted on June 23, 2020. Courts found no personal enrichment.

Sandiganbayan · Rappler · Philstar

Why it matters

Her 2004–2010 term is the only break in Dy-family rule of Isabela in modern times. The family reclaimed every provincial and Cauayan executive seat in 2010 and has held them since — a case study in how hard dynasty alternation is to keep.

PCIJ · VERA Files · Rappler

Quick facts

Born Oct 25, 1963 Cauayan, Isabela · polio at 3, lifelong crutch user
Career before politics 14 yrs Bombo Radyo DZNC Cauayan, 1986–2000 · CPA
Governor 2004–2010 Two terms, served in full despite a post-hoc annulment
2008 Ramon Magsaysay Award Government Service — Asia’s premier civilian honor

The backdrop: three decades of Dy rule

To weigh the Padaca years you need the era they interrupted. The Dy family took the governorship in the late 1960s and — except for her six years — has never lost it. Her predecessor was Faustino Dy Jr. (1998–2004); her successors from 2010 come from the same family, led by Faustino “Bojie” Dy III.

Before — Dy Jr., 1998–2004

  • Continuation of family rule dating to the late 1960s; in 2004, 33 of the province’s mayors backed the machine against her.
  • Documented criticisms of the era, from her broadcasts and press commentary: jueteng rackets, illegal logging, and a stagnating rural economy.
  • Provincial finances were in poor shape — the Magsaysay Foundation later wrote that she inherited and paid off “huge debts.”
  • The 2001 Dy-vs-Padaca congressional count was contested all the way to the House electoral tribunal.
Bulatlat 2004 · RMAF · HRET

The break — Padaca, 2004–2010

  • Won 2004 with ~55% on a “Free Isabela” slogan, ending ~35 years of Dy rule.
  • Re-elected 2007; that win was annulled by Comelec in Dec 2009 after a partial recount — but the reversal never took effect and she served the full term.
  • Lost the 2010 race by 3,438 votes; the Dy restoration began the next day.
  • Governed against a hostile provincial board and most mayors — see the criticisms.
RMAF · Philstar · GMA · VERA Files

After — Dy restoration, 2010–present

  • Bojie Dy III took the governorship back in 2010 and the family has held every provincial and Cauayan executive seat since.
  • Her comeback attempts failed decisively: 2016 governor (lost by 300,000+), 2019 vice governor (lost by 316,420).
  • Dy III later rose to Speaker of the House of Representatives; Rappler’s 2026 profile describes the monopoly as “cracked only twice” — 2004 and 2007, both by Padaca.
GMA · Rappler · Inquirer

Recognition

  • Ramon Magsaysay Award for Government Service, 2008 — cited for “empowering Isabela voters to reclaim their democratic right to elect leaders of their own choosing.” The Senate passed a resolution commending her.
  • International Women of Courage, reported 2007 — Philippine press documented US Ambassador Kristie Kenney conferring the honor on Dec 5, 2007. Caveat: the US State Department’s own official 2007 honoree list (ten women, eight countries) does not include her; treat this as Philippine-press-documented, not confirmed in State Dept records.
  • Kaya Natin! — co-founded the good-governance movement with Among Ed Panlilio, Jesse Robredo, and Sonia Lorenzo; its “Islands of Hope” campus tour launched in 2008.
RMAF · Senate · GMA

Programs that defined the term

  • Hybrid rice & farmer credit — the flagship program whose credit arm later became the criminal case (see the courts).
  • Health restructure — replaced a bankrupt free-medicine scheme with PhilHealth subsidies; doubled barangay health-worker allowances; gave public attorneys a ₱2,000/month allowance.
  • Forest protection — reactivated the anti-illegal-logging task force and created a task force for the Northern Sierra Madre Natural Park.
  • Fiscal cleanup — overhauled provincial finances, neutralized early sabotage by Dy loyalists inside the bureaucracy (per the Magsaysay biography).
RMAF · PCIJ · Rappler

The criticisms

A fair reading includes the record’s other side. These are documented criticisms — mostly from 2005–2016 journalism (PCIJ, VERA Files, Philstar, ABS-CBN) — not allegations this page endorses.

Governing alone

  • Placed eight division chiefs on floating status as Dy loyalists; the Civil Service Commission ordered seven reinstated.
  • A provincial board member said she implemented none of the board’s resolutions; PCIJ reported she couldn’t convene the school, health, and peace-and-order boards.
  • Constituents grumbled that “the capitol has become a university” — her meticulous scrutiny of fund releases demanded written proposals and photos with every check.
PCIJ

The mayors’ revolt & the 2005 recall

  • None of the incumbent mayors supported her in 2004; she bypassed them and worked directly with barangays.
  • Retaliation: several mayors stopped remitting real property taxes — over ₱26M in 2005.
  • Ten months in, a recall drive spread across 25 towns; it never materialized into an election.
PCIJ · Philstar

The anti-logging backlash

  • Enforcement put thousands of bogadors (timber haulers) out of work, with no livelihood transition.
  • VERA Files ties this to her margin shrinking from ~44,000 (2004) to ~17,000 (2007); ABS-CBN argued the logging drive cost her rural votes in 2010.
  • The core critique of her signature achievement: enforcement without livelihoods.
VERA Files · ABS-CBN

Jueteng: the promise she couldn’t keep

  • Declared “ayoko ng jueteng” (I don’t want jueteng) at her inauguration, then accused mayors and police of taking payoffs — alongside Archbishop Oscar Cruz.
  • She did not eradicate it. The provincial police chief publicly rebutted her, saying jueteng was already “crippled by 85 percent”; she blamed national inaction: “You just need the President to close the faucet.”
  • A rumor that her brother took payoffs circulated; she dismissed it as gossip — documented in reporting only as a rumor.
PCIJ · Philstar · VERA Files

The court cases

The same hybrid-rice program that made her reputation became a criminal case. This section reports what the courts decided — including the conviction — and what remains unresolved. It draws on the Sandiganbayan’s own June 23, 2020 resolution.

Red flags the courts and auditors cited: the MOA was signed January 8, 2006 — before the provincial board authorized the NGO engagement (January 31, 2006), and the board ratified the MOA only in January 2007, after COA asked; the agreement had no repayment scheme; the NGO never submitted financial statements; the provincial accountant who flagged all this was overruled.

Sandiganbayan 2020 resolution · COA · Rappler · Philstar

What she was convicted of

On November 15, 2019, the Sandiganbayan (Third Division) convicted her of:

  • Malversation of public funds — 12–14 years. Upheld: her motion for reconsideration was denied on June 23, 2020.
  • Graft — 6–10 years, originally. Acquitted on June 23, 2020 (below).
  • Plus: a ₱18M fine, ₱18M restitution with 6% interest, and perpetual disqualification from public office.
  • Separately, in 2017 she pleaded guilty to four counts of not filing her SALN (2007–2010) and was fined ₱4,000.
Rappler · Philstar · GMA

What she was acquitted of

On June 23, 2020 — a single, 32-page resolution — the Sandiganbayan:

  • Reversed the graft conviction: “failure of the prosecution to prove her guilt beyond reasonable doubt.” The charge alleged dispensing with public bidding, but the evidence proved a different failing — convicting on facts not alleged would violate her constitutional right to be informed of the accusation.
  • Lifted the hold-departure order and ordered her bail bond returned.
  • But denied her reconsideration on malversation.
Sandiganbayan 2020 resolution · Philstar · GMA · Inquirer

Reading the verdict fairly

What the conviction is

  • A public-funds-controls case: she was found negligent for letting an NGO hold ₱18M with no repayment scheme and no financial reporting.
  • Her own courtroom statement: “With the ₱25 million, I didn’t personally gain even one cent. That all went to the farmers of Isabela.”
  • Status as of September 2026: the malversation conviction stands at the Sandiganbayan level; she remains free pending further appeal, and no ruling above the Sandiganbayan has been documented. Her lawyers said in 2019 they would appeal “all the way to the Supreme Court.”

What it is not — and her defense

  • No personal enrichment was found — by the courts or, in its findings, the Ombudsman. No credible source alleges she pocketed money.
  • She says the case was politically orchestrated — the original complaint was filed in 2007 by a Dy-era politician; the Dys denied involvement. Both positions are on the record; this page treats orchestration as her allegation, not established fact.
  • Her co-accused (a provincial legal officer and two NGO officers) all died before judgment; their cases were dismissed.
Rappler · Philstar · SC e-library · Inquirer

After the governorship

Comelec commissioner, 2012–2014

Appointed by President Aquino (sworn in October 8, 2012). The Commission on Appointments never confirmed her — she was bypassed in 2014, reportedly over the pending case — and the appointment lapsed when Congress recessed. She blamed LP politics, including Mar Roxas, for the abandonment.

Rappler · Inquirer · Philstar

Comebacks that failed

2016, governor: lost to Dy III by 300,000+ — allies defected to the majority party days before filing. 2019, vice governor: allied with one Dy brother against another; the family reconciled, her running mate withdrew, and she lost 483,392 to 166,972.

Rappler · Philstar · Inquirer

Today

Per a March 2026 profile, she is a speaker and leadership trainer, leading a Metro Manila chapter of the Christian leadership network Haggai International and championing leaders with disabilities. No electoral activity is documented since 2019.

How to weigh it

For voters and researchers, the honest ledger has three columns.

The case for her record

  • Verifiable outcomes: debt down two-thirds, health coverage up to ~700,000, record logging enforcement.
  • International recognition (Magsaysay Award) predates the case against her.
  • The courts themselves found no personal enrichment — and acquitted the graft count.
  • She remains the only figure to twice break a five-decade dynasty at the ballot.

The case against

  • A standing Sandiganbayan-level malversation conviction: ₱18M of public money unaccounted under her watch, with her own accountant’s warnings overruled.
  • Governing style alienated the mayors, the board, and parts of her base — the recall drive, the tax standoff, the margin collapse from 44K to 17K.
  • Signature promises unkept on jueteng; signature enforcement (logging) imposed real livelihood costs she did not replace.
  • The SALN admissions — four years of disclosure failures.

Genuinely open questions

  • Will the malversation conviction survive appeal? No ruling above the Sandiganbayan is documented as of September 2026.
  • The 2007 annulment appeal was never formally resolved — her term simply ended with the motion pending.
  • The 2004 margin (+44,292) is the best-attested figure, but no raw 2004 tally has been located to verify it.
  • The 2019 conviction decision text itself is not public — the sentence facts rest on five agreeing press accounts.

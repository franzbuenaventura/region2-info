## identity
- name: City of Santiago
- slug: santiago
- class: Independent Component City (geographically in Isabela; administratively independent of the provincial government — the only ICC in Region 2)
- nicknames: "Queen City of the North", "Premier Investment Hub of the North", Regional Center for Trade and Commerce
- founded: May 4, 1743 (as pueblo "Carig" on the old Carig River, now Diadi River); municipality 1910; cityhood July 6, 1994 (RA 7720)
- etymology: renamed for St. James the Great (Santiago/Saint James), patron saint; original Gaddang/Ibanag settlement was "Carig"
- district: 4th legislative district of Isabela (co-located; Santiago votes with the 4th district for Congress)

## points (Points tab — unique characteristics)
- ONLY independent component city in Region 2 — administratively independent of Isabela province (1994 RA 7720; briefly downgraded to component city 1998; restored by Supreme Court decision)
- First city in Region 2 (cityhood 1994 predates Tuguegarao 1999, Cauayan 2001, Ilagan 2012)
- "Queen City of the North" / Regional Center for Trade and Commerce — southern crossroads of Cagayan Valley (Isabela-Quirino-Nueva Vizcaya junction)
- Robinsons Santiago — first full Robinsons department store in Region 2
- Balay na Santiago heritage house; St. James the Apostle Parish Church; Calao steel-arch bridge (Pan-American Hwy heritage)
- Regional healthcare gravity: SIMC referral hospital for Isabela, Nueva Vizcaya, Quirino, northern Aurora
- Land use: dense urban core on Maharlika corridor; agricultural fringe (corn/rice); highest elevation 919 m (foothills toward Quirino)
- Flood/typhoon: Super Typhoon Uwan (Nov 2025) hit Isabela province-wide (33 towns dark); Santiago's southern valleys (Diadi/Carig rivers) flood in extreme events — verify barangay-level mapping
- Seismic: Santiago Segment fault (Phivolcs, M7.2 potential) runs in the province — site-level check via Phivolcs FaultFinder
- Security: Cagayan Valley declared insurgency-free (RPOC Jun 2026); Santiago = commercial core, stable

### Historical politics (timeline in Political tab)
- 1743: founded as pueblo Carig (Gaddang/Ibanag settlement on the Carig/Diadi River banks); renamed for St. James the Great
- Spanish era: gobernadorcillos (Callapag, Alvarez, Bayaua, Carreon, Lumidao, Alvarez — CPDO list)
- 1910: re-established as a full municipality (had been reverted to a barangay of Echague)
- 1945+: postwar growth via Pan-American (Maharlika) Highway; Calao steel-arch bridge
- May 5, 1994: RA 7720 converts Santiago into an INDEPENDENT COMPONENT CITY (first city of Region 2); Jose C. Miranda = first city mayor (1994–1998)
- 1998–1999: Joel G. Miranda mayor (died in office Oct 10, 1999); Amelita S. Navarro succeeds (Oct 10, 1999 – Jun 30, 2001)
- 1998: legally transformed into a component city; a historic Supreme Court decision later RESTORED independent-component status (cityofsantiago.gov.ph history; RA 7720 annulment controversy era)
- 1999–2013: Amelita S. Navarro dominates (1999–2001 holdover, 2001 re-elected, 2004, 2007, 2010 — 2004 upset over the city's "political Goliath" per Philstar)
- 2013–2022: Joseph "Jojo" Tan wins three consecutive terms (2013, 2016, 2019 — the 2019 race vs returning ex-mayor Amelita Navarro, who lost 31,571 to his 40,747 after withdrawing her congressional bid); his daughter Alyssa Sheena Tan succeeds him in 2022 at 48.97% (PDP-Laban)
- 2025: Alyssa Sheena Tan re-elected 71.88% landslide (Lakas); running-mate Jamayne Tan wins VM; Tan/Dy machine holds 9 of 10 council seats
- Dynasty map: Tan-Dy machine (mayor+congress+VM) vs Miranda clan (former mayors Jose/Joel/Jigs/Otep; Aksyon) vs Navarro legacy (NPC) — Santiago's politics are family-coalition politics with real but fragmented opposition

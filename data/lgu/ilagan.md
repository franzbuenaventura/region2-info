## points (Points tab — unique characteristics)
- "Corn Capital of the Philippines" (designated Aug 11, 2015): ~200,000 MT corn/yr off 33,500 ha — the biggest corn area of any PH city; plus ~80,000 MT rice/yr from 15,000 ha
- Largest city on Luzon by land area (1,166.26 km²; 4th largest PH city by area after Davao, Puerto Princesa, and one other)
- World's Largest Wooden Lounge Chair (Butaka) landmark; ILAGAN Sanctuary (zoo/garden/nature park); Ilagan Japanese Tunnel (WWII, Brgy Sto. Tomas); Musical Dancing Fountains; Queen Isabela Park
- Provincial capital: hosts provincial capitol, ILAGANDA (Ilagan Development Authority, 2019 — "liveable city by 2030" vision)
- Hosted Bagong Pilipinas Serbisyo Fair (Nov 25–26, 2023) — biggest national services caravan at the time (₱500M services, 100K recipients, 26 agencies)
- Sports Tourism Hub of the North: City of Ilagan Sports Complex + City Sports and Convention Center
- Flood profile: Super Typhoon Uwan (Nov 2025) — 132 barangays flooded region-wide; 28 of Ilagan's 91 barangays submerged, 5,000+ families affected, ~400 families isolated (GMA/ANC); flood risk is the city's top physical risk
- Seismic: Isabela crossed by 3 active faults (Phivolcs Jun 2026); unnamed Ilagan fault produced a 500-tremor swarm (Jun 2025) — verify site-level via Phivolcs FaultFinder
- Security: Cagayan Valley declared insurgency-free (RPOC Jun 2026); Ilagan = administrative center, stable

### Historical politics (timeline in Political tab)
- 1581/1686: Gaddang settlement "Bolo" on the Cagayan River tobacco country; May 4, 1686 founded as Dominican mission town; capital of Cagayan Valley under Gov. Manuel Sanchez Mira; 1763 Dabo/Juan Marayag revolt vs tribute & tobacco monopoly
- 1856: capital town of the new province of Isabela de Luzon (May 1, 1856)
- 1901–1930s: Americans re-establish Isabela (Act 210, Aug 4, 1901); Ilagan's first municipal president Rafael Maramag becomes the province's first governor; Ilagan re-confirmed as provincial capital
- 1945: liberated June 19, 1945 (USAFIP-NL + US 37th Division); Ilagan Japanese Tunnel built by forced labor — now a tourist site
- Postwar: poblacion moved uphill from St. Ferdinand Parish (Bagumbayan) to San Vicente — current City Hall site
- 1998–1999: first cityhood attempt — RA 8474 passed Feb 2, 1998, but the March 14, 1999 plebiscite FAILED (voters said no) under Mayor Mercedes P. Uy
- 2001–2006: Delfinito Calimag Albano (of the Albano clan) elected mayor 2001 and 2004; assassinated in Quezon City June 27, 2006; VM Josemarie Diaz finishes the term
- 2007–2012: Diaz elected 2007 and 2010; second cityhood campaign (HB 5917, "C-U-DAD Ilagan"), bill signed by President Aquino June 21, 2012
- Aug 11, 2012: PLEBISCITE WON — Ilagan proclaimed a component city by COMELEC Commissioner Armando Velasco (RA 10169); 3rd city of Isabela, 4th of Cagayan Valley; Diaz is the first city mayor (2013–2016)
- 2016–2019: Evelyn C. Diaz (wife) serves one term as mayor — the couple alternated; Diaz returns 2019 and holds the seat since
- 2019–present: ILAGANDA created (Aug 28, 2019); 2023 Serbisyo Fair; 2025 Diaz landslide + son Jayve as VM; Albano dynasty holds Congress (Tonypet unopposed 2025)
- Dynasty map: Diaz machine (city hall, PFP) + Albano dynasty (congress, Lakas) coexist; Uy clan (Mercedes Pua Uy 1992–2001, Bonifacio Uy 1986–89) is the older lineage

The 2012 proclamation date (Aug 11) is well-attested; the "first city mayor" question (Diaz 2012 holdover vs 2013 election) depends on source.
